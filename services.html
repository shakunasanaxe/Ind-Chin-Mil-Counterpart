Command and control · 03

Eight forces on one side, three and some agencies on the other

The PLA now has four services (Army, Navy, Air Force, Rocket Force) and four arms (Aerospace, Cyberspace, Information Support, Joint Logistics Support). India has three services, plus a set of tri-service commands and agencies built up over twenty years. The comparison is clearest when the question is asked in terms of status: who has a seat at the apex, and who sits in a staff.

What to take from this page

01

Status, not only function, separates the two systems

The Chinese Cyberspace and Aerospace Forces are theatre-grade arms. Their Indian counterparts, the Defence Cyber Agency and the Defence Space Agency, are headed by two-star officers inside a joint staff.

02

The Rocket Force has no direct Indian equivalent

It is a full service that holds both nuclear and conventional missiles. India's nuclear delivery forces are organised as the tri-service Strategic Forces Command under the Nuclear Command Authority.

03

Logistics is joint in China, and still service-bound in India

The Joint Logistics Support Force, with its headquarters in Wuhan and five regional centres, serves all theatres. India's Joint Logistics Nodes are a start, but small.

04

The Army lost its dominance in China

Before 2016 the Army had no headquarters of its own because the general departments, staffed mostly by army officers, ran the whole force. The reform gave it a headquarters and put it on a par with the others.

05

Paramilitary forces answer to different masters

The People's Armed Police and the Coast Guard report to the CMC. India's central armed police forces report to the Home Ministry, and the Coast Guard to the Defence Ministry.

06

Reserves differ in scale and purpose

The Chinese militia and reserve system is integrated with provincial Party committees and includes a maritime militia. The Indian Territorial Army is small and organised within the Army.
