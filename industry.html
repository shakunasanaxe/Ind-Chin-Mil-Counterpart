Who designs, who builds and who buys

India separates the work among services (who set requirements), DRDO (who design), public and private firms (who build), the DGQA (who inspect) and the Defence Acquisition Council and the CCS (who approve). China concentrates most of it in a single military buyer, the Equipment Development Department, and a handful of state-owned groups that design and build in-house. What to take from this page

01

Integration against separation

Chinese groups design and build under one roof, which shortens the path from idea to platform. India separates the roles, which gives more checks and more handovers.

02

A single buyer has costs too

The Equipment Development Department has been at the centre of corruption inquiries, and its dominance may have contributed to the scale of the problem.

03

The Indian base is shifting

The corporatisation of the Ordnance Factory Board in 2021 and the growth of private participation are changing the balance, with negative import lists pushing orders toward domestic firms.

04

The nuclear complex sits apart in both

India's Department of Atomic Energy and China's CNNC are outside the ordinary military procurement chain, which is deliberate.

05

Military-civil fusion has no exact equivalent

India encourages private firms and start-ups through iDEX. China's strategy is wider and tries to bring civilian technology, universities and finance into a single national effort.

06

Counting firms is not counting capacity

The nine Chinese groups are enormous, and the sixteen Indian DPSUs are comparatively small. Compare scale before comparing numbers.
