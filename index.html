Scale and structure

Five numbers, with the caveats attached

Headline numbers are the usual way to open a comparison, and they are worth knowing, but they tend to hide how differently the two systems are put together. The first three measures below are about size and resources. The last two are about organisation, and they say more about why the comparison is hard.

Three differences that organise the rest

What to keep in mind on every page

01

The Party sits inside the army

Every Chinese unit has a Party committee and a political commissar of equal standing with the commander. Indian civilian control works from outside the force, through the ministry and the Cabinet. The two answer the same question in structurally opposite ways.
02

Joint command exists in one and is pending in the other

China has fought through five theatres since 2016. India has seventeen service commands and two joint ones, and its plan for integrated theatres has slipped past its first target. Many of the other differences follow from this one.
03

Design and production under one roof, or separated

Chinese state groups design, build and often export under a single management. India divides requirement, design, production, inspection and approval among separate bodies, and is now reorganising the production end.

How to read the charts

Three kinds of answer

● Close counterpart. The two bodies do much the same job at much the same level, though a note will say where they differ.

○ Partial overlap. The two share some functions, usually because one system has combined what the other keeps separate.

No marker. Nothing on the other side performs the function in a comparable form. The panel explains why, since the absence usually says something about how the system is built.

Every box also carries a status: established, reported, or in flux. Treat the last two as provisional.
