Command and control · 04

Five theatres operate; three are still on paper in India

Since 1 February 2016 the PLA has fought through five joint theatre commands, each with a commander and a political commissar and a staff drawn from all services. India's commands are still organised by service. The schematic below is a cartogram and not a map: it shows the approximate position of each command, and the structure beneath it can be explored in the paired chart.

What to take from this page

01

A working system and a draft

China has run five joint theatres for ten years, and has had time to find their weaknesses. India's plan for three theatres has been under discussion for a long time, with an expected first command in 2026 that did not materialise.

02

The Western Theatre is the one that matters for India

It faces the whole Line of Actual Control, which three Indian Army commands and three Air commands currently cover. The proposed Northern Theatre is designed to answer it, and that is the most direct pairing in this page.

03

Theatre commanders do not own the forces

The services build, train and equip. Theatres fight. The Rocket Force and the three new arms stay outside the theatres and may be placed in support of them.

04

Commanders and commissars are posts

Each theatre has a commander and a political commissar of equal grade, which is the pairing to set against an Indian Army Commander. The charts show these as posts and note where the former holder has been removed.

05

Joint command was not the only aim

The 2016 reform also cut the army's dominance, broke up the old regions that had built local power bases, and tightened the CMC's hold on operational command.

06

The count of headquarters misleads

India has seventeen service commands, plus two joint ones. China has five. A smaller number of joint headquarters is not the same as fewer layers, but it is a simpler chain of command.
