A hundred years of organisation, two columns

The two militaries took different routes to their present shapes. China's changes arrived in large, centrally directed waves in 1985, 2015-16 and 2024. India's arrived more slowly and more often after a crisis, such as the 1962 war and Kargil. Use the filters to read one country at a time, or read across to see how their reforms line up.

What to take from this page

01

China reforms from the top, and in waves

The 1985, 2015-16 and 2024 changes were each decided by the central leadership and implemented over a short period. The scale of the reform has no Indian equivalent.

02

India reforms after a shock

Kargil produced the IDS, the DIA and the Andaman and Nicobar Command. The CDS arrived two decades after the first recommendation, and the theatre commands remain outstanding.

03

Reform separates three lines of authority

Since 2016 the CMC has managed, the theatres have fought and the services have built, as three distinct lines. That separation is the benchmark against which India's theatre proposal is usually measured.

04

The two sides have borrowed from each other's mistakes

The Rudra brigades, for example, echo the Chinese combined-arms brigade, and the dissolution of the SSF shows that China is still adjusting its own model.

05

Timing matters

The Chinese theatres date from 2016 and the Indian proposal has been open for more than twenty years. The comparison is partly a comparison of institutional speed.

06

Dates are as reported

Entries for 2025 and 2026 rest on press and think-tank reporting. Check them against official sources if they matter to your argument.
