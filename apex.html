Command and control · 01

Who stands above the armed forces

In India the authority over the armed forces is spread across a President who commands in name, a Cabinet committee that approves, a ministry that administers and a uniformed staff that advises. In China the Central Military Commission gathers most of those roles in a single Party body, which is why the Chinese ministry of defence looks so thin beside the Indian one.

What to take from this page

01

The CMC has no single Indian counterpart

It overlaps with the Cabinet Committee on Security (political authority), the Ministry of Defence (administration), the Department of Military Affairs (uniformed staff) and the Chiefs of Staff Committee (coordination). A student who looks for one box will not find it.

02

Two ministries of defence, two different jobs

The Indian ministry sets policy, holds the budget and runs acquisition. The Chinese Ministry of National Defense is an external-facing window, and the minister was a CMC member only until 2023.

03

Civilian oversight takes opposite forms

India places civilians in the chain: the Raksha Mantri, the CCS and the civil service in the ministry. China places the Party in the chain, through commissars and Party committees. Both claim control, and they achieve it by different means.

04

Structure outlasts its occupants

The CMC is designed around a Chairman, vice-chairmen and the heads of a few key organs. The comparison here is between those roles and their Indian counterparts, so it holds whether the commission has two members or ten.

05

Nuclear authority is split differently

India has an explicit civilian Political Council and an Executive Council under the NSA. China's public documents place nuclear forces under the CMC and the Rocket Force, without a comparable separate civilian council.

06

Parliament and the NPC are not mirror images

Indian Parliament scrutinises the defence budget and has a standing committee. The NPC ratifies appointments and the Party decides in advance.
