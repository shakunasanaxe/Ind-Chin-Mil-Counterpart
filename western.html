The Western Theatre, unit by unit

The Western Theatre Command in Chengdu is the single Chinese command that faces India along the whole of the Line of Actual Control. It has two group armies, two military districts that double as border and internal security organisations, a theatre air force on plateau airfields, and a set of quasi-civilian bodies that have no Indian parallel. Facing it, India has three Army commands, three Air commands and two paramilitary organisations under the Home Ministry.

Reading the front

Three Indian commands answer one Chinese one

The Indian Army's Northern, Central and Eastern Commands, with the Western, Central and Eastern Air Commands, each cover a slice of the front that the Western Theatre covers as a whole. Decisions about reinforcement across those slices run through the Army and Air Force headquarters in Delhi, and any cross-service coordination runs through ad hoc arrangements or the Chiefs of Staff Committee. A Northern Theatre Command would collect them under one commander, which is the logic behind the proposal.

Several features of the Chinese side stand out to a student of Indian defence: the political commissar of equal grade at every level, the district structure that ties the force to civil government in Tibet and Xinjiang, the dual-use border villages and roads, and a paramilitary corps that takes over internal security. Each of these is a feature of the Chinese state as much as of its army.

What to take from this page

01

One theatre, many roles

The Western Theatre faces India, Pakistan and Central Asia, and also contains Xinjiang and Tibet, where the state's internal security concerns are acute. That combination shapes its force structure.

02

The districts are not like Indian commands

The Tibet and Xinjiang Military Districts are territorial organisations with border defence duties and links to Party committees. They are a mix of an Indian command, a state government's security arm and a border force.

03

Border infrastructure is part of the order of battle

Highways, the Lhasa-Nyingchi railway, hardened airfields and the xiaokang villages are treated by both sides as part of the military balance. India's equivalent is the BRO and the Vibrant Villages Programme.

04

The border guard is a police force in India

The ITBP sits under the Home Ministry, whereas Chinese border defence regiments are in the military chain. This matters for how quickly each side can move from guarding to fighting.

05

A fixed order of battle

The group armies, military districts, air bases and logistics centre described here are established, and they are what the Indian Northern, Central and Eastern Commands are compared against.

06

Compare the posts

The theatre commander and political commissar are the roles to set against an Indian Army Commander and the proposed theatre commander. The structure is what carries the comparison.
